# STP Segmentation Targeting Positioning

## Purpose
Use this skill to decide whom to serve and how to be perceived.

## When to use
- The user names STP Segmentation Targeting Positioning explicitly.
- The task matches: decide whom to serve and how to be perceived.

## Before you start
Confirm or ask for: the company/market/context, the decision being made, the time horizon, and any available data. If data is missing, state assumptions explicitly and label them.

## Process
1. Segment: choose bases (demographic, behavioral, needs) and build distinct segments.
2. Evaluate segments: size, growth, profitability, accessibility, fit.
3. Target: select primary and secondary segments.
4. Position: craft a positioning statement and perceptual map versus competitors.
5. Define the proof points and the marketing implications.

## Output format
Segment table, target choice, and positioning statement (For [target], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit] because [reason]).
Lead with the answer or key insight first, then supporting detail. Keep the structure MECE and quantify wherever possible.

## Common pitfalls
Segments that are not actionable; targeting everyone.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Conclusions are evidence-based and sources or assumptions are stated.
- [ ] Output ends with clear, prioritized recommendations ("so what").
- [ ] Categories do not overlap and nothing material is missing.
