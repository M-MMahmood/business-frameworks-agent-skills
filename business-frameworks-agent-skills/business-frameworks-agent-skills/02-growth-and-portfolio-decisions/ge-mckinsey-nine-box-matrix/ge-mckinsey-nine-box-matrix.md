# GE-McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix

## Purpose
Use this skill to prioritize investment across business units using industry attractiveness and competitive strength.

## When to use
- The user names GE-McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix explicitly.
- The task matches: prioritize investment across business units using industry attractiveness and competitive strength.

## Before you start
Confirm or ask for: the company/market/context, the decision being made, the time horizon, and any available data. If data is missing, state assumptions explicitly and label them.

## Process
1. Define criteria and weights for industry attractiveness (size, growth, margins, intensity) and competitive strength (share, brand, cost, capabilities).
2. Score each unit 1-5 and compute weighted scores.
3. Place units on the 3x3 grid.
4. Apply guidance: top-left invest/grow, diagonal selective, bottom-right harvest/divest.
5. Recommend investment priorities and review triggers.

## Output format
Scoring table, grid placement, and strategic stance per unit.
Lead with the answer or key insight first, then supporting detail. Keep the structure MECE and quantify wherever possible.

## Common pitfalls
Unweighted or subjective scoring without evidence; ignoring trajectory.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Conclusions are evidence-based and sources or assumptions are stated.
- [ ] Output ends with clear, prioritized recommendations ("so what").
- [ ] Categories do not overlap and nothing material is missing.
