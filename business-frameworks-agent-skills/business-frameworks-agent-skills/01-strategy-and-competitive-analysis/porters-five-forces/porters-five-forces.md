# Porter's Five Forces

## Purpose
Use this skill to assess industry attractiveness and profit potential.

## When to use
- The user names Porter's Five Forces explicitly.
- The task matches: assess industry attractiveness and profit potential.

## Before you start
Confirm or ask for: the company/market/context, the decision being made, the time horizon, and any available data. If data is missing, state assumptions explicitly and label them.

## Process
1. Define the industry and geographic scope precisely.
2. Rate each force Low/Medium/High with evidence: threat of new entrants (barriers, capital, scale, regulation), supplier power (concentration, switching costs), buyer power (concentration, price sensitivity), threat of substitutes (price-performance of alternatives), rivalry (growth, concentration, exit barriers).
3. Identify the 1-2 dominant forces driving profitability.
4. Assess how forces are shifting (technology, regulation, consolidation).
5. Derive strategic implications: where to position, what to defend, what to influence.

## Output format
Force-by-force table (rating, evidence, trend), overall attractiveness verdict, and 3 strategic implications.
Lead with the answer or key insight first, then supporting detail. Keep the structure MECE and quantify wherever possible.

## Common pitfalls
Defining the industry too broadly; listing factors without rating them; ignoring complements and trends.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Conclusions are evidence-based and sources or assumptions are stated.
- [ ] Output ends with clear, prioritized recommendations ("so what").
- [ ] Categories do not overlap and nothing material is missing.
